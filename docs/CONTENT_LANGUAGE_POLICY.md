# Content Language Policy

Editorial principles for language on Internet Culture Hub.

This is **not** a banned-word filter. Judgment stays with editors. The goal is accurate documentation of internet culture without turning encyclopedia prose into needless shock, insult, or harassment.

---

## Core principle

Document the culture as it exists. Do not sanitize history or slang out of existence.

Words that are historically or culturally important — including edgy, vulgar, or controversial terms — may appear when they are the subject, a necessary quotation, or required for accurate definition.

---

## What encyclopedia prose should avoid

In ordinary editorial writing (descriptions, explanations, captions, UI copy):

- Unnecessary profanity used for spice rather than accuracy
- Insults aimed at readers or real people
- Harassment framing or call-outs that encourage abuse
- Slurs used casually or for shock value
- Hate speech or content that endorses harm against protected groups
- Sexually explicit description when a neutral summary would suffice

Tone of voice and “edgy brand” are not reasons to break these rules.

---

## When offensive terms belong

If an offensive term **is the topic** (or is inseparable from the topic):

1. State the term clearly enough that readers know what is being documented.
2. Describe it **neutrally**, with cultural and historical context.
3. Explain usage, audience, and harm where relevant — without moralizing lectures or euphemism that obscures meaning.
4. Prefer primary documentation over gossip.
5. Do not amplify the term through gratuitous repetition in headlines, alt text, or social blurbs when a clear reference will do.

Example approach: define the term, note communities and eras that used it, note controversy or harm if well-sourced, then move on.

---

## Quotations and examples

- Usage examples may include coarse internet speech when that is how the term actually appears.
- Prefer examples that teach meaning over examples that only provoke.
- Do not invent slurs or hate phrases for “color.”

---

## Creators and living people

- Stick to documented public facts and widely reported events.
- Avoid speculative attacks on character.
- Separate the cultural phenomenon from personal vilification.

---

## What we do not build

- No automated swear-word blocklists that break legitimate articles
- No shadow “safe mode” that silently rewrites definitions
- No fake neutrality that erases documented bigotry or harm when explaining a term’s history

Editors apply this policy; validation scripts check structure and media, not a vocabulary blacklist.
